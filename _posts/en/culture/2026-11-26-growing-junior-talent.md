*AI-Native Culture 07*

## Where Do First Experiences Come From?

If AI can handle basic coding and document cleanup, what work should a junior be given? Reducing repetitive work is a good thing, but the concern that the experiences needed to become skilled will disappear along with it deserves serious treatment.

At the same time, we do not think that banning AI for juniors only is the best answer. Learning fundamentals and using tools in real work are different purposes, and they can be designed together.

## AI May Narrow the Skill Gap

The 2025 study of 5,172 customer service employees published in the QJE found that the productivity effect of AI support averaged 15%, and was relatively larger for employees with less experience and skill. The study also reported evidence of a potential effect on learning.[1] At the same time, the outcome of a service job cannot be equated with long-term software engineering education.

A 2023 study by Harvard Business School and BCG shows that work AI performs well and work it does not coexist.[2] In a learning process, it matters that even when AI gives a fast answer, the person learns the limits of that answer and how to verify it.

## Use AI as a Learning Partner, Not an Answer Sheet

For example, an engineer implementing an API for the first time can receive complete code from AI. But the learning goal is not to copy and run it. The person should be able to explain why that data structure was chosen, where authentication happens, and what response a failed request should receive.

First write your own hypothesis, then ask AI for alternatives. After implementing, design tests, and deliberately change input conditions to trace failure causes. Finally, explain what you understood to a colleague. That process helps prevent thinking from being skipped without banning AI.

## A Sample 90-Day Growth Program

| Period | Learning goal | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1 to 30 | Fundamentals, tools, security policy | Small implementation and explanation |
| Days 31 to 60 | Real work and AI collaboration | Code review, evidence and tests |
| Days 61 to 90 | Independent problem solving and responsibility | Bounded mission and retrospective |

The table is an example and should be adjusted to the role and the individual's experience. When evaluating a junior's growth, look at finding errors, the judgment to ask for help, how feedback is applied, and knowledge sharing, not throughput alone.

Shopify's official culture post introduces a case where AI experimentation and sharing of learning are encouraged at the organizational level.[3] Anthropic's hiring guidance also distinguishes improving expression with AI from replacing the candidate's own thinking.[4] The same distinction is useful in education.

## The Senior Role Changes Too

A senior can extend from passing on answers to teaching the structure of a problem and its verification criteria. Make how you review AI's output explicit, analyze failure cases together, and design the scope so a junior can attempt a hard problem safely.

The organization must provide approved tools, a practice environment, and mentoring time. If AI use is left only to individual ability and individual cost, the gap in learning opportunities can widen.

What we want is neither a junior who can do nothing without AI nor one who never uses it. We want a person who develops their own expertise, uses tools to solve harder problems, and can explain the results. Designing the opportunity to grow is the company's responsibility.
