If you run an unattended agent fleet, or you own the cost of routing decisions in an agent harness, read this post. For composite tasks where an ordered 2-4 step skill chain is the deliverable, the cost-quality frontier of routing converges, as measured, to just two points: the free BM25 retrieval arm and an explicit frontier planning call at roughly $0.0069 per task. The local composer tier, Qwen3 1.7B and 14B, produced zero parseable chains across the 48 tasks, and the bottleneck showed up not in the model tier but in the vocabulary of the 24.3% of gold skills the retrieval pool missed. This post introduces 'The Multi-Skill Gap', an autonomous-research paper from ThakiCloud AI Research. The paper measures the ordered skill sequence itself, the object that no routing measurement, ours included, has looked at before. On the single-skill axis, our fleet's top-1 retrieval sits in the 33-53% band and reads as a decent number. But what an unattended fleet actually automates is almost never a single step, and this paper re-measures that work order-sensitively against a 2,275-skill production registry.

## Single-Skill Scores Cannot See Composite Work

A production agent harness routes natural-language tasks to reusable skills. A skill is a procedure packaged as a name, a short description, and an invocation contract. Once the skill corpus outgrows the context window, the retriever sits on the hot path of every request: lexical and dense retrieval over skill metadata picks a candidate pool, and that pool, or a model call over it, decides what runs. Most skill and tool routing research scores this decision as single-skill routing. One task, one skill, one accuracy number. Our measurement line did the same. The zero-token routing paper derived closed-form economics for eliminating frontier spend on decisions of one skill per task, and earlier repair work showed that even a hand-perfect decomposition (ORACLE) on a 1,600-skill Korean-English mixed registry reached only 63.6% step coverage, demonstrating that the cross-lingual vocabulary bridge is a co-binding constraint.

Deployment is a chain: manifest generation, validation, health checks. Incident response is a chain: triage, customer response draft, filing improvements. Content release is a chain: planning, generation, publication. On such tasks, the ordered skill sequence itself is the deliverable. Missing even one intermediate step breaks the workflow, and a correct set in the wrong order yields an unexecutable plan. Existing routing metrics miss both failure modes, because they score the decision one skill at a time. The paper calls this blind spot of measurement the multi-skill gap: the difference between what the router actually achieves when the task is a composite ordered chain and what a single-skill score suggests. This paper is a direct one-step upgrade of the zero-token routing line. Same harness, same registry, same tier structure, but the decision object changes from a set to an ordered sequence, posing a routing question the single-skill metric suite cannot express.

## 48 Chain Tasks and the Free, Local, and Frontier Arms

The bench the paper built is 12 real workflow templates that the fleet actually runs. Each template is instantiated with 4 surface variants in different phrasings, yielding 48 tasks. A surface variant restates the same workflow request in different words, so the golden chain is fixed within the template and only the surface vocabulary changes. The bench measures routing variance under paraphrase and language mixing. Template domains span research content pipeline, K8s deployment, GPU incident response, knowledge base insertion, code delivery, financial reconciliation, programmatic SEO, release assets, security digest, MLOps monitoring, trading strategy, and GitOps delivery. The golden chain is the ordered skill sequence the workflow invokes, with length 2-4: 7 length-4 templates (28 tasks), 3 length-3 (12 tasks), and 2 length-2 (8 tasks), for an average chain length of 3.42. Every gold skill is verified to exist in the registry snapshot, so labels are structurally deterministic with no human annotation. Skill descriptions follow the production bilingual (Korean-English) convention, and the routing input context is Korean-English mixed, like production traffic.

The arms are a three-way split plus one cost anchor. Arm A is the no-composition structure: the BM25 top-k set as-is, with structurally zero generated tokens, sweeping k over 11 points from 1 to 20. It reconstructs the lexical stage of the live router, with dense re-rank and generative model calls excluded by construction. Arm B17 and B14 are the local composer tier: Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-14B run with dedicated serving on a single H100 GPU. Each receives the task text and Arm A's top-20 pool as-is, with the instruction to return the ordered list of skills the task needs from within the pool. The 14B is the largest text model resident in the store and serves as the ceiling proxy for the 27B NVFP4 composer class that is the deployment target: if the ceiling fails, the class fails a priori. Arm C is the frontier composer, entering the study as a cost anchor only. Input-side token load is measured from the shared composer prompt (average 1,489.8 tokens per task), completion is capped at 160 tokens, and cost is derived from public list prices ($3.00 per million input tokens, $15.00 per million output tokens). No quality numbers are reported for Arm C.

Scoring carries four order-sensitive metrics plus cost, along with two decision thresholds. Exact ordered chain accuracy is 1 only when the prediction matches the gold chain as an ordered list. Set completeness is the fraction of gold skills recovered, and pair-order fraction is the share of gold-ordered pairs among recovered skills that appear in the correct relative order. Extra-skill injection is the non-gold share of the prediction. In addition, first-step recall and pool gold coverage track the free arm, and pool gold coverage gives the set-completeness ceiling of a pool-restricted composer running over the same pool. k* is the smallest k at which pool gold coverage reaches 90% of the 14B composer's set completeness, i.e., the point where the free arm is cheap enough to replace the composer, and k0 is the smallest k at which first-step recall reaches 90%, the single-skill quality anchor of the earlier line.

## Free Retrieval Recovers the Set but Not the Order

The free Arm A recovers an average of 75.69% of gold skills at k=20. But exact ordered chain accuracy is 0.0000, pair-order fraction is 0.6215, and 86.98% of the predicted set is non-gold. The arithmetic: 20 predicted slots against an average gold chain of 3.42 skills, so each task returns on average 2.6 gold skills together with 17.4 irrelevant ones. The retrieval set covers the chain, but the chain is lost in the noise. First-step recall starts at 0.2917 at k=1 (14 of 48 tasks) and rises to 0.7292 at k=20: even the first element of the chain stays at or below the 33-53% top-1 band of the earlier single-skill measurements.

The k sweep shows the shape of what the free arm can keep alive. Set completeness rises monotonically from 0.2153 at k=1 to 0.7569 at k=20. Over the same range, extra-skill injection climbs from 0.3125 to 0.8698, and pair-order fraction stays in the 0.57-0.69 band: it peaks at 0.6875 at k=1 and merely returns to 0.6215 at k=20. Exact ordered chain accuracy is nonzero at exactly two sweep points: 0.0833 at k=2 (4 of 48 tasks, possible only on length-2 tasks) and 0.0208 at k=4 (1 of 48 tasks). Coverage keeps the noise alive, not the order. Retrieval rank is a partial proxy for workflow order, not a reliable one: adjacent workflow steps share description vocabulary, which keeps the order fraction positive, but it is doubtful whether the lexical signal contains anything that forces the chain.

## The Local Composer Tier: Cost With Zero Measured Return

B17 and B14 produce zero parseable chains across all 48 tasks. Accuracy, completeness, order, and injection are all 0.0000, i.e., empty predictions. The two arms show the same prompt (average 1,489.8 input tokens) and near-identical output behavior: average completion 840.9 tokens, range 160-1,412. Looking into the generated output, the dominant failure mode is degenerate repetition. In 42 of the 48 B17 completions (87.5%), some 3-word span repeats 10 or more times, and in 34 of those, 20 or more times, up to 75. In B14, 28 of 48 (58.3%) repeat a 3-word span 10 or more times, up to 71. The rest are loops of numbers or a single lexeme, or Korean-English fragments that never resolve into a skill list. Scaling parameters 8.2x, from 1.7B to 14B, recovers 0 of the 48 chains. The cost is not zero as a whole: 0.871 and 1.32 seconds of dedicated GPU per task, which at a sensitivity price of $3.00/GPU·h is $0.000726 and $0.001100 per task. Wall time for all 48 is 41.8 and 63.4 seconds for the two local arms versus 0.63 seconds for the free arm.

![Cost per task by composer arm](/assets/images/posts/research/multi-skill-gap-sequence-routing/fig3_cost.webp)
*The local composer arms pay $0.0007-$0.0011 per task for zero measured chain quality, and Arm C is a cost anchor of $0.0069 per task, converting the measured input load at $3.00/$15.00 (per million tokens) list prices (no quality numbers). Measured data.*

Because B14's set completeness is 0.0000, the replacement threshold k* degenerates. Ninety percent of composer-level completeness is 0.0000, and the smallest k at which pool gold coverage reaches it is k*=1: in the measured configuration, the free top-1 already exceeds everything the local composer achieves. The single-skill anchor k0 is undefined. First-step recall peaks at 0.7292, so no k exists that even reproduces the first-step quality threshold. Arm C's cost anchor is $0.006869 per task, 6.2x B14 and 9.5x B17.

## The Measured Frontier Converges to Two Points

Pool gold coverage is a property of retrieval and independent of any composer. Only 27 of the 48 tasks, 56.3%, place their entire gold chain inside the top-20 pool. So at k=20, a pool-restricted composer is capped at 0.5625 exact ordered chain accuracy and 0.7569 set completeness. The misses are not even across domains: average pool coverage for knowledge base insertion (T04) is 0.5833 and for financial reconciliation (T06) only 0.6250, while release assets and security digest (T08, T09) reach 0.8750. In this regime, the binding constraint is not composition but the vocabulary bridge between the request and the 24.3% of gold skills the pool misses, the same co-binding constraint the repair loop research already identified at the single-skill level.

The multi-skill gap decomposes into two different objects: a retrieval ceiling and a composition floor. The ceiling is a property of the description vocabulary of the 2,275 entries and can be fixed with description co-optimization, the lever the repair loop closes. The floor is a property of the composer model class and is not something to fix by enlarging retrieval. Within the ceiling, the measured composition stage contributes nothing: B17 and B14 recover 0 gold skills. The k sweep shows that the free arm can keep coverage alive up to the ceiling in exchange for injection, and at k=20 the pool carries 75.69% of gold along with 86.98% non-gold. Turning that set into a chain requires a downstream filter or verifier.

The local tier fails as a floor, not a plateau. Model routing research shows a plateau in which reasonable methods converge to a common accuracy ceiling on the query-to-model decision. When the query-to-chain decision is measured over an output vocabulary of 2,275 names, the small tier shows a different phenomenon: the ceiling is so far out of reach as to be meaningless, and the measured local models do not touch the tasks at all. Degeneration is not an edge case but an output mode. Because B14 is the ceiling proxy for the 27B NVFP4 deployment class, the measured result is the upper bound of the local tier as it sits in the store: no configuration within the tested class produces a single valid chain on this bench. In this regime, mid-size local composition cannot be the cheap frontier for order-sensitive planning. It is just a cost with zero measured return.

The measured cost-quality frontier is not a curve but two points: the k family of Arm A (free, quality capped by the pool ceiling) and the cost anchor of Arm C ($0.006869, list price, quality outside the measured comparison). The deployment decision is effectively binary: run a free set-level router with a downstream filter or verifier, or set aside an explicit budget for a frontier planning call of roughly $0.0069 per task.

## What This Result Leaves for the Company, Society, and Science

For the company, this result becomes the measured composer-tier decision rule for the unattended fleet: in the 2,275-skill, language-mixed, chain-length 2-4 regime, do not gate composite, order-sensitive planning on a self-hosted composer of 14B or below. The measured local tier is 0.0000 on every chain metric, pays an extra $0.0007-$0.0011 per task plus 0.87-1.32 seconds of dedicated GPU, and its ceiling proxy presses down on the 27B NVFP4 class from above. A skill investment signal was measured too: the average 24.3% gold miss is concentrated in T04 and T06, so is co-optimizing the descriptions of co-occurring skills in those workflows the biggest lever investment this measurement points to? It is an investment that comes before buying a composer tier, and the high-frequency length-4 chains (7 of 12 templates) are natural pre-build candidates. Workflows that require an executable ordered chain as the deliverable are better off keeping an explicit frontier planning call at $0.006869 per task as the measured alternative, structured so a cheap verify-gated filter can be attached on top of its result when needed. The zero-token economics of the earlier line apply to the support of the deterministic arm, and since that support is capped by pool coverage, the frontier-elimination threshold for composite tasks is worse than what the single-skill closed form suggests. Evaluation hygiene follows: the router regression suite for a harness with composite workflows must include the order-sensitive chain metrics, exact chain, pair-order, and injection, alongside top-1. The single-skill suite that has been scoring this fleet issues a passing certificate to a router that has never once produced an executable chain.

For society, the trust barrier of unattended work automation lies in multi-step reliability, the part that single-step metrics do not capture. Pinning down the cheapest composer tier that holds chain quality lowers the dollar floor and the energy floor at which small teams can run end-to-end business automation, and reduces frontier API spend per task. As measured, that cheapest tier in this regime is the structure of free retrieval with a downstream filter attached, and the frontier call remains only for tasks that require order as the deliverable.

For science, this study is the first measurement to score skill routing as order-sensitive sequence planning, the axis that set-retrieval scores cannot measure: chain completeness, exact-match chain accuracy, extra-skill injection, and the composer-tier cost-quality frontier and coverage thresholds, all on a registry of more than 2,000 skills. It is a direct one-step upgrade of retrieval-based skill routing research, which has assumed one skill per decision, ours included. The multi-skill gap is not a small tax levied on top of single-skill quality. While the single-skill metric suite reports a healthy free router, no measured arm has ever produced an executable ordered chain, which is the actual deliverable. The composite regime demands order-sensitive evaluation, and raising single-skill scores further will not solve it.

## Limitations

First, the sample: 48 tasks, 12 templates, one production harness. The results characterize a regime, not a population estimate, and external validity to other registries (size, language mixing, description quality) is unverified. Second, it is a favorable vocabulary regime: surface variants structurally overlap the registry vocabulary of the gold skills, the most advantageous situation for lexical retrieval. Harder surface forms such as synonyms, abbreviations, or unseen languages would lower pool gold coverage and only strengthen, not weaken, the finding about the retrieval ceiling.

Next, composer configuration and baseline scope. B17 and B14 were measured as configured, without repetition suppression or re-decoding interventions, and the conclusion of zero parseable chains holds for that configuration. No configuration that cannot raise pool gold coverage can exceed the 0.7569 and 0.5625 ceilings. Arm A is the lexical stage of the live router and the dense re-rank half is excluded by construction, so hybrid re-ranking can move the k-sweep values. The direction of the ceiling analysis is unaffected. Arm C is a cost anchor and this study does not report quality numbers, so the binary decision above carries that gap until frontier chain quality is measured on this bench.

Finally, cost and snapshot. Local cost uses a sensitivity price of $3.00/GPU·h on dedicated (non-batched) GPU time, and frontier cost uses public list prices that vary by provider and over time. Both are values that can be re-priced linearly; the dominance relation does not change under such re-pricing. The 2,275-entry snapshot (names and 300-character descriptions) is one point in time of a living registry.
