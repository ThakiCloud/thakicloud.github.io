This week's news is img2threejs, an open-source tool that builds a 3D scene from a single photo. The digital twin it produces is a virtual replica of a real facility. The premise is simple: render what the photo showed. But a 3D scene contains spaces the photo never captured, like the backs of objects and the insides of walls. An inference-based tool fills those gaps with guesses. This comic shows what happens when a data center photo goes out to the cloud and comes back infinite.

## What this means for ThakiCloud

The joke is a joke, but the structure is real. Running an inference tool like image-to-3D means the source photo leaves the building. What comes back is a replica of your facility that a model guessed, a 3D map more detailed than reality. At ThakiCloud the same class of tools runs inside the customer's own facility: Paxis runs the agents and Metis runs the inference, so the photo never leaves and the guess never comes back. A digital twin is only as trustworthy as the data that made it. That is why on-prem is a quality spec here, not a slogan.
