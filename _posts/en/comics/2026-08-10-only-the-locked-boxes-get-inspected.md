Washington's pre-release safety review lands on closed frontier AI: models whose weights stay private and reach you only through an API. Open-weight models, the ones anyone can download and crack open, sit this round out.
So the asymmetry is this. The box you cannot open gets opened by a regulator on your behalf. The box everyone can open, nobody bothers to.
Which leaves the third case. A model that runs only inside your own building never joins the queue, because it is never released to anyone. The upside and the catch are the same sentence: the only inspector it will ever get is you.

## What this means for ThakiCloud

For an enterprise the news reduces to one question: who can open your model, and where does that get written down.
ThakiCloud's answer is Aegis, which keeps the weights and the data inside your own facility, with Paxis running the agent work in that same perimeter. Nothing ships outward, so no external review calendar owns your release date. The trade is that you become the inspector, which is why Signum carries the permissions and the audit trail for every model call.
Safety someone else certifies once and safety you look at every morning are not the same product. Teams who pick on-prem have usually already picked the second one.
