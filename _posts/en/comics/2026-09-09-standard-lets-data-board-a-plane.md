Korea's national agency is pushing a revision to its cloud security standard, and the debate is whether public data could end up under the management of US or Chinese cloud providers sitting overseas. A cloud security standard is simply the government rule that decides where public data may be stored and who runs it. The worry is that the revision widens that 'where' — and counts data moving abroad as 'safe.' This comic turns that controversy into a small story about characters who tried to shrink the standard down to a single line.

## What this means for ThakiCloud

The moment a security standard allows 'safe wherever it sits,' the real question becomes where the data actually is. So we tried shrinking the standard to one line: keep public data from leaving, period. That is sovereign on-prem — models, data and infrastructure all running inside our own facility, under our own control. Paxis spins up agents inside that wall, and Metis handles the learning and inference without ever handing the data to someone else's meter. The Jarvis orchestrator that builds this very blog runs on the same principle, calling its agents automatically on a system whose data never crosses a wall. When you narrow the standard so nothing leaves, what you end up with is control, not just safety.
